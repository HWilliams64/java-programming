Module 04 Discussion

Lists, sets, and maps in everyday work

Discussion Topic

Lists, sets, and maps in everyday work

Discussion Prompt

Choose a real collection of information from everyday life or your field. Describe a task that uses that information, then explain whether order, duplicates, or key-based lookup matter. Choose an array, list, set, or map for the task and justify the choice. Give a small example with at least three entries, including a repeated value or a missing lookup. Explain the result your design should produce, and connect it to a module example.

Context and Purpose

A volunteer schedule needs an order, a list of unique supplies should avoid repeated item names, and an inventory associates each item with a quantity. The same starting information may need different structures for different tasks.

This discussion practices choosing a representation for a stated purpose. Use ordinary language so classmates can check how your example handles order, repetition, and missing information.

Requirements and Logistics

  • Describe the information and the task, then justify your chosen structure.
  • Connect your choice to a specific array, list, set, or map notebook example or video.
  • Give at least three entries and trace a repeated-value or missing-lookup case; Java code is optional.
  • In a classmate reply, test an additional input or explain how a different ordering or duplicate rule would change the choice.
  • Use the accompanying Canvas discussion. Follow the instructor’s posted due dates and participation requirements; the table below guides feedback.
